Limitations

  • Research and teaching prototype; not clinically validated and not approved for any clinical use. It does not provide diagnosis, grading, prognosis, treatment or surgical/radiotherapy planning.
  • The pretrained model was trained by the MONAI team on BraTS 2018 (adult glioma, pre-operative, skull-stripped, co-registered, 1 mm). It may fail on other tumour types (metastases, meningioma, paediatric tumours), post-operative scans, non-skull-stripped or non-co-registered images, unusual contrast, motion or other artefacts.
  • Evaluation cases come from the Medical Segmentation Decathlon Task01, which derives from BraTS 2016/2017; most are likely included in the model's BraTS 2018 training data, so reported Dice/sensitivity are optimistic.
  • Enhancing tumour (ET) and necrotic core (NCR) are small, low-contrast regions and are the least reliable; ET can be missed entirely or predicted where an expert would mark none (in our evaluation, non-enhancing tumours such as BRATS_175 and BRATS_169 received spurious ET/TC predictions).
  • Images coarser than 1 mm are resampled to 1 mm; thick-slice data reduces accuracy and volume precision.
  • Volumes are estimates (voxel count x voxel volume) of the model's predicted regions, not measurements of the tumour, and inherit all segmentation errors.
  • Input suitability checks are simple heuristics (background fraction, tissue volume, mask agreement, size, shape, texture); passing them does not guarantee the scan is appropriate.
  • The test-time-augmentation uncertainty only measures how the prediction changes when the scan is mirrored; a confidently wrong region can be unanimous across passes. Modality ablation is not clinical evidence.
